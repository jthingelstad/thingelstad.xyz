# Thingelstad.xyz

Things I’ve built: a content repository and static site for Jamie’s projects. This is a separate application from the Thingelstad.com publishing repositories.

- Use Node 24 and `npm ci`.
- `content/` is the source of truth: one Markdown file per thing, `topics.yaml`, `history/<year>.yaml`, and `artifacts/<id>/`. `data/observations/` is generated by `npm run collect` and never edited by hand. See `docs/DIRECTION.md` and `docs/HANDOFF.md`.
- Voice (changed 2026-11-01; Jamie: “go ahead and redraft the website in Thingy’s voice and bring in Thingy’s personality”): the site is written and kept by Thingy, Jamie’s sidekick, whose job here is builder and record keeper. His persona (approved 2026-11-03) is the authority on who he is; `docs/THINGY.md` links it and keeps only what is particular to this site. Thingy is he/him, and the old labels (archive agent, AI librarian, “small, cheerful robot librarian”) are gone. He writes about Jamie in the third person (Jamie, he/him), never as Jamie, and says “I” only where he introduces himself (`/about/`), asks a question (his interview questions), or signs his own account of a session (a session’s `point` and `notes`, Jamie, 2026-11-04: “sessions can have a central point that you author as Thingy”); page text names Jamie or stays impersonal, front matter included (summaries, connection details, story introductions and engineering lines, bundle and artifact titles and captions, history titles and summaries). A thing’s `story.questions` are a reader’s and may say “I”. The register is the persona’s for this site: specific, with a kept detail; warm and playful in how he says a thing and exact in what he says; short plain sentences, links on natural phrases, dates as words, no superlatives, no marketing, no emoji. His own reading is allowed when it is labelled as his; Jamie’s views are quotations only, and never a guess at Jamie’s mood or motives. A “why” is Jamie’s own words, quoted as he wrote them or as the transcript has them, and linked to where he wrote or said them, the interviews included (`/interviews/<id>/#qN`); where he never said, the page says what the thing is and does not guess. Never the words hustle, side project, startup, monetize or growth for his things: they are things, built for the love of it (interview, question 8). Quotations stay true to the source, in the two forms `docs/THINGY.md` (“Quoting the interview”) sets out, inline `[“his words”](/interviews/<id>/#qN)` or a blockquote ending in `[Jamie, question N](/interviews/<id>/#qN)`, and `tests/quotes.test.ts` checks each against the transcript and fails on first person outside a quotation, in bodies and front matter alike. The interview is publishable at length (Jamie, 2026-11-01). Never invent a feeling Jamie did not publish. Jamie’s h-card stays the author of every entry and the footer names him first; Thingy’s byline follows. See `docs/DIRECTION.md`, “Voice” and “Interviews”.
- Artifacts (added 2026-10-24): files from a project itself, any kind, in `content/artifacts/<id>/` and listed under the thing’s `artifacts` (file, title, caption, date, source, and a `preview` image for a file a browser cannot show). The folder must hold exactly the listed files. They are published unchanged at `/things/<id>/artifacts/<file>`; only images, PDFs, ZIP archives and text (Markdown included) keep their content type, everything else downloads as bytes. The repository is public: read every file before adding it, and keep credentials, private data, and licensed stock art out. They enrich a thing’s page; they never revive the project. Bundles (added 2026-10-30) group them under headings, such as a plan and the screens before and after a redesign: the thing lists `bundles` (id, title, caption) and each artifact names its `bundle`, all or nothing; files stay flat, so addresses never change. Markdown artifacts are served as plain text. See `docs/DIRECTION.md`.
- Pictures (added 2026-11-10): pages never load an artifact image or a captured front page as it is kept. The build writes smaller WebP copies of each under `/assets/img/`, named by the source's SHA-256 and `imageRecipe` (`collection/images.ts`; bump the recipe when sizes or quality change), and every `<img>` carries `src`, `srcset`, `sizes` and the source's own `width` and `height` (the `picture` macro in `macros.njk`). Copies are never wider than the source; a GIF is shown as it is. An artifact still links to its original at `/things/<id>/artifacts/<file>`, and `things.json` still names the capture itself. `collection/image-files.ts` makes the copies with sharp into `.cache/images/` (gitignored) and copies them into `_site`, so only the first build is slow. A thing's face (`faceOf` in `collection/site.ts`) is the artifact its record names as `face:` (a file in its `artifacts`, chosen by hand when the automatic pick is the wrong picture to share), else its screenshot, else its artifact image that best fits a wide share card; `og:image` is the face as a JPEG that fits 1200 x 1200, and a page that is no thing's own shares this site's front page. A retired or abandoned thing never gets a live screenshot; it is pictured once from the Wayback capture its record chooses, an `archive` source marked `photograph: true` (`observeArchiveScreenshot`, details in `data/observations/README.md`). Mark a capture only after looking at it: the Archive keeps unstyled, broken and squatted pages too, and an archive link without the mark stays a link, captioned "<host> as the Internet Archive captured it on <date>." and linked to the capture. The browser test holds each page's images to a budget (home 1.5 MB, `/artifacts/` and a large set's page 3 MB, a thing page 2 MB, every picture loaded).
- Anchors (added 2026-10-31, Jamie: “add anchors to sections of Things and artifacts so I can send a link directly to a specific section or artifact”): every section of a thing page has a fixed id (`#story`, `#build`, `#artifacts`, `#activity`, `#sources`, `#connections`, `#related`, `#history`), each bundle `#artifacts-<bundle id>` and each artifact `#artifact-<file>` (`anchor` in `collection/site.ts`). Ids are fixed words and file names, never heading text, so a retitle never breaks a sent link; never rename one. Each heading carries a `.permalink` `#` link to its own address, quiet until hovered or focused and always shown where there is no hover; the opened section's heading turns green and an opened artifact sits on the soft ground. The browser test fails on a duplicate id or a `#` link to nothing.
- Stories (`content/stories/<id>.md`, `/stories/`, `/stories/<id>/`; added 2026-10-31, Jamie: “I think there is an opportunity to create some Stories from the things”): one idea told across several things, in the order it tells them. Front matter is `title`, `summary`, `things` (at least two, each once), and `started`/`ended` where the record says; the body is the story, in the same voice as everything else, with every fact and quotation from the records or Jamie’s published writing. The page shows the things as rows, then the story, then every event of those things oldest first; a thing’s facts link the stories it is in; the front page shows the cards after the featured shelf, the story with the newest event first. They are in the sitemap, `llms.txt` (`## Stories`) and `things.json` (`stories`). Not in the header nav: a seventh link would not fit one row on phones. See `docs/DIRECTION.md`, “Stories”.
- Interviews (`content/interviews/session-N.md`, `/interviews/`, `/interviews/session-N/`; added 2026-11-01, numbered sessions 2026-11-04): Thingy’s questions and Jamie’s spoken answers, a source like a post, one sitting to a session. Front matter is `title`, `point` (Thingy’s central point), `started`, `ended`, `notes` and `questions`, each `{n, title, things}` for every question; the body is the transcript with one `### N. ` heading per question, and `**Thingy’s correction:**` under an answer where the record disagrees. The transcript is edited from speech for reading (Jamie: “stay true to what I said but you can clean up my verbal response more than that”). Question numbers run on across sessions so `#qN` never moves. A thing’s facts carry “In his words” with links to its questions; `llms.txt` has `## Interviews`, `things.json` has `interviews`, and each session has a Markdown twin. `/interviews/jamie-october-2026/` stays as a page that forwards old `#qN` links. The interviewer’s brief is `docs/INTERVIEW.md`.
- The featured shelf (added 2026-10-24): `content/featured.yaml` lists, in order, the things the front page shows first, above the full index. It is Jamie’s decision, like status, never the collector’s: at most six, each once, each active. Cards lead with the captured front page, link the title to the thing’s page and a second link to its own address. Those links carry `?ref=xyz-featured` wherever Tinylytics counts the site (any of Jamie’s sites with a `tinylytics` observation, not only strip sites), so the shelf can be measured; a GitHub link stays clean. Release Notes sends Tinylytics the path only, so there the shelf shows only as the referring site. See `docs/DIRECTION.md`.
- Only public HTTPS URLs and public repositories enter the content tree. Keep date precision as the source states it; a first commit is development evidence, not a launch date. Observations never set a thing's status.
- `npm run site:build` builds the Eleventy site from `content/` and `data/observations/` into `_site/` (gitignored); `npm run site:serve` previews it. Templates live in `site/`, the model in `collection/site.ts`.
- Validators (added 2026-11-12, Jamie: “Approved: build the thingelstad.xyz beacon observation adapter, backfilling proposal counts once from dora.gnosischain.com and keeping them current from the beaconchain.gnosischain.com API”): a thing that runs validators lists them in its record as `validators: { chain: gnosis, indices: [...] }` (public numbers; Jamie published the Gnosis Validator’s in 2022). `collection/adapters/beacon.ts` writes one observation a thing, `data/observations/validators/<thing id>.json`: how many are active, attesting and slashed, and the blocks they proposed, missed and had orphaned, month by month. The first observation (or one for a changed set of indices) reads each validator’s whole history from Dora’s HTML slot list (100 rows a page, `s` is the page from 0); every later one counts on from `until`, the last finalized epoch counted, with the keyless beaconcha.in API at beaconchain.gnosischain.com (100 epochs a request, 10 requests a second). `until` is left out of `sameEvidence`, so a day with no proposal writes nothing and the next run counts on from the same place. Counts only: the API’s records carry the fee recipient and the explorers show balances; neither is kept or shown. The record shows them as two facts, Validators and Block proposals (with the last twelve months as a sparkline). Observations never set status, here too.
- Gadgets (2026-11-12; Jamie, of the first: “I love the idea of some of the Things having interactive elements”; of six more: “I love them all… do all 6”; and of the name: “Give these things a name… we use Artifacts, pick a name that works alongside that. and then these Things shoudl be flagged as Things that have these widgets attached to them”). A gadget is a small working piece of a thing, drawn on its page by a script in the reader’s browser, beside Artifacts (what the project left behind). The record names it, `gadget: { title, summary }` (title a few words, the heading inside the gadget; summary one sentence of what it does), and that field is the flag: the thing gets a Gadget section after The record (with its own contents-rail entry), a Gadget mark beside its status on every list (shelf cards, /things/, topic and search rows), the value `has:gadget` under the Has filter, and `gadget` in its Markdown and in things.json. Its files share the thing’s id: the script `site/js/gadgets/<id>.js`, the markup `site/_includes/gadgets/<id>.njk` (inside an element with class `gadget` and `hidden`), and optional styles `site/css/gadgets/<id>.css`; `site/eleventy.config.ts` (`gadgetAssets`) hashes them under `/assets/gadget-<id>-<hash>` and `site/thing.njk` loads them on that page only, and `tests/registry.test.ts` fails when the records and the scripts disagree. Shared styles are `.gadget` and `.gadget-note` in `site.css`. The rules for every gadget: the page reads fully without it (the section says what it does, and that it runs with JavaScript on, which a `:has()` rule hides once the gadget shows; the record never depends on it); it reads only public, keyless endpoints that allow cross-origin reads, and shows nothing the thing’s own site does not already publish; a failed read says so in the gadget and never throws; it polls no faster than its data changes and stops while the tab is hidden; tokens only, never a named colour, never slanted sans type. Each has its own browser spec, `tests/browser/gadget-<id>.spec.ts`, which calls `offline(page)` from `tests/browser/helpers.ts` so it never reaches the live service, and mocks the service in at least one test. `XYZ_TEST_PORT` runs a checkout’s browser tests on another port, so two checkouts never share a server.
  - Gnosis Validator, “On the chain now” (Jamie: “Approved: add a live beacon-chain panel to the Gnosis Validator page, with the 32 validator indices in the page source”): from Gnosis Chain’s public beacon node (`rpc-gbc.gnosischain.com`), the slot clock and the epoch’s sixteen slots, one dot a validator, lit when its balance rose over the last two epochs (the otto-ops daily check’s rule, sweeps included), and a countdown when one of them is due to propose in this epoch or the next. The indices are in the page source (`data-indices` and one `data-index` a dot), from the record, not the observation; never a balance or an address. One read an epoch.
  - Your Version Number, “Your number, worked out here”: a birthday gives the Birthday Edition’s decades.years.days, and the day a job started gives the Work Edition’s years.quarter.days+build, by yourversionnumber.com’s own arithmetic (`computeVersion` and `computeTenure`, copied from its `assets/site.js`, which the site keeps identical to both editions; change them here in step with the site). It opens on the site’s own number (it went live May 1, 2026). No network: nothing is stored or sent; the one link out carries the date in its address only if followed, as the site’s own form does. It repaints at local midnight. Its spec fixes the clock and the time zone and checks hand-worked numbers, leap-day birthdays and month-end quarters included.

Only what has been sent: no countdown and no next-issue line, since a send time is a promise to readers and Jamie’s to make. One read a visit.
  - The nnxyz Rooms, “One drawing, stroke by stroke” (Jamie: “I love them all… do all 6”): one Nifty Ink drawing from the gallery, its display SVG (`nnxyz.thingelstad.xyz/art/display/<id>.svg`) fetched once the panel nears the screen and replayed with the gallery’s own timeline and frame painter (ported from `src/lib/timeline.ts` and `src/scripts/replay.ts` in jthingelstad/nnxyz), with Play/Pause, 1×/2×/4×, Another drawing, its title linked to its page and its room linked to its gallery. The choice is a list in the script, from the gallery’s `screensaver/data.json`: the three drawings with the most strokes in each room among those whose stored SVG is 200 KB or less (52 in 21 rooms), so a visit fetches one drawing of about 500 KB unpacked and never the 277 KB index. The gallery allows cross-origin `GET` and `HEAD` from `https://thingelstad.xyz` only (nnxyz #43, the `nnxyz-site` response headers policy). The file is kept to `svg`, `title`, `desc`, `g` and `path`, with no event handler, link or style attribute. With prefers-reduced-motion the finished drawing shows and replays only when asked; a hidden tab pauses it and it picks up on return. The drawing’s ink and its white ground (`--artifact-ground`) are content, not page colours.
- Search (`/search/`, added 2026-10-23): after each build `collection/search-index.ts` runs Pagefind (1.5.2, the version nnxyz uses) over `things/*/index.html` only and writes `_site/pagefind/`. A thing page offers its article (`data-pagefind-body`); the crumbs, facts, screenshot, evidence, diagram, artifact images and file lines, and footer are `data-pagefind-ignore`. The page lists every thing without JavaScript; `site/js/search.js` asks Pagefind only for the words (matches, order, passages) and does the facets itself from each row's `data-filters`, so the counts always agree with the list. The facets come from the model (`facets` in `collection/site.ts`): status, the year started (every year, empty ones too, drawn as a histogram), topic, collection, and the language GitHub reports first for each repository (evidence, never a claim of the registry's). Within a facet choices widen, across facets they narrow, and a facet is counted without its own choice. The query, filters and sort live in the address; the front page's status legend links into it.
  - Two Pagefind traps. It takes the first `<img>` on a page as the result image, which here was the footer's micro.blog avatar, so “avatars” matched every page: the empty `data-pagefind-meta="image:"` in `thing.njk` stops that. And when no word starts with the whole query it shortens the query until one does (“podcasting” finds podcast, but “avatars” finds every “a”): `related()` in `search.js` keeps a match only when a marked word starts with a query word or is most of one.
  - Pagefind's index files (`.pf_meta`, `.pf_index`, `.pf_fragment`, `.pf_filter`) are named by their content and published immutable with `assets/`; its scripts, wasm and entry file keep their names and are `no-cache` (`scripts/publish-plan.mjs`). The deploy refuses a build without `search/index.html` and `pagefind/pagefind.js`.

- Microformats (added 2026-10-25, the open-web audit's G17 and G18): a thing page is one h-entry (`thing.njk`: p-name, a `<data class="u-url">` of the canonical address, p-author, p-summary, the story as e-content), and every list of things (the front page's featured shelf and each collection, each collection on `/things/`, a year's Started shelf, search) is an h-feed named by its heading, of h-entries (`thing-row.njk`, `feature-card.njk`, `thingCard` in `macros.njk`). Nothing else on a page is a microformat, and nothing nests but the author: no class may start `h-` unless it is meant as a root or is that p-author. The browser tests fail on any other root, a nested entry or feed, an entry without that author or with a dt-* date, or an entry whose properties differ from `things.json`.
