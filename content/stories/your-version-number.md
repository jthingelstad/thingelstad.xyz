Jamie’s friend David Hussman gave his age as a version number. In February 2018 Jamie wrote it up as [Your Version Number](https://www.thingelstad.com/2018/02/24/your-version-number.html) and mapped it onto semantic versioning: decades are the major version, years into the decade the minor, and days since your last birthday the patch. He was v4.6.52 that day. David died that August. The arithmetic is his, and the [site’s About page](https://yourversionnumber.com/about/) says so.

> the idea came from a conversation I was having with my friend. David Hussman, and he was, he and I were talking about this idea of software versions and talking about like, you know, having a major version, a minor version, and every day. And we were just riffing on this idea and really both like just jamming on like it being a really fun concept.
>
> [Jamie, question 12](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q12)

For years the idea lived in a Workflow script, and then a [shortcut on his phone](https://www.thingelstad.com/2025/02/25/jamie.html). In May 2026 he made it a website with Claude Code, shared in [Weekly Thing 346](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/346/) and then in [a launch post](https://www.thingelstad.com/2026/05/06/your-version-number-the-website.html). Everything lives in the URL, so a view can be bookmarked or sent to someone, with no account. A Work Edition does the same arithmetic for a job. It is, he said, an idea [“that started as a seed and has refused to go away”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q5).

After making the site he wished he got his version by email, and then wondered what it would be like to add notes to each version. So he built it. [Release Notes](https://www.thingelstad.com/2026/10/08/in-i-wrote-about-your.html) started on October 8, 2026. Each morning an email says “You’re 5.4.278 today,” and whatever you reply becomes that version’s release notes. It is lightweight journaling built around the reply, with a writing streak, “On this day” notes from earlier releases, tags and search, photos and audio, and an API that lets agents in with the owner’s say-so. [“I just wanted to get an email every day that had my version number.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q19)

Three weeks later, on October 29, 2026, he built [Changelog](https://thingelstad.xyz/things/changelog/) in a day: an iPhone app for getting notes into Release Notes in two taps, with photos, voice memos, places, Siri and widgets. It numbers itself the way Release Notes numbers people. It was, in his words, [“my very first attempt at an iPhone app”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q19).

One idea from a friend, eight years of carrying it around, and then three things in six months.
