Road Sign Math started with one sign. Driving home from North Dakota in 2005 Jamie passed a sign on I-94 that read Melrose 3, St Cloud 33, Minneapolis 99, and 3 × 33 = 99. His friend Jim told him he should make a website, [“and so that day I did a website.”](https://www.thingelstad.com/projects/open-loop/1-big-fuzzy-dead-cat/) That was [February 13, 2005](https://www.thingelstad.com/2010/02/13/010000.html).

> I would see this sign. And it was always this sign that said 33399. And to me, it was like just obvious that you should. Go up to the sign, put a time sign and an equal sign, because here’s this sign that was essentially a math equation just sitting right there. And I saw that sign dozens of times and thought, like, that’s so funny. And then I think, like, I wonder how many other signs there are like that.
>
> [Jamie, question 9](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q9)

The first site ran for eleven years. It was a blog of signs, then a WordPress site with rules, a scoring system and scoreboards, and in 2011 a wiki so players could keep their own signs. Forty players posted 251 signs from 44 states and eight other countries, one of them in the Serengeti. Jim Bernard and Suzie Meredith made him a poster of the first year’s signs. Back in 2011 he thought about an iPhone app for capturing signs, but never built it. In 2016, during a round of [technical downsizing](https://www.thingelstad.com/2016/05/03/technical-downsizing.html), he shut the site down and let the domain expire. Looking back, he calls the scoring the wrong turn: [“the very first version is almost one of those stories about a rabbit hole that I went down and took a wrong turn. Because I kind of gamified it.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q10)

That was not the end of it. In March 2021 Tammy found a sign near Phoenix, and Road Sign Math got [“a breath of new life”](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/177/) as a blog of just the family’s signs. That summer they found ten more on a [road trip through Vermont](https://www.thingelstad.com/2021/08/15/varmont-road-trip.html). In October 2023 he [rebooted it again](https://www.thingelstad.com/2023/10/21/road-sign-math.html) on Micro.blog at roadsignmath.xyz: “Just my signs. No scoring system or leaderboards.” Sixteen signs over five years. Not many, but as he wrote when one turned up in 2025, [it always makes him smile](https://www.thingelstad.com/2025/09/01/it-always-makes-me-smile.html).

> So the second version was a direct response to us making a three-day road trip to Arizona. And Tammy made these envelopes of activities that the kids could do to keep them busy. One of them was to play Road Sign Math, and there was a prize if they were able to find signs. And it was just, I loved it because it brought this thing that I had built back into our family. … So I decided to bring it back with a blog and just keep it simple and focus on just having fun.
>
> [Jamie, question 10](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q10)

Then, in October 2026, the third reboot. The iPhone app he had wanted since 2011 exists, built with coding agents. You photograph a sign from the passenger seat, the app reads the numbers, you make the math true, and you post it. The sixteen signs from the blog became its first sixteen. [roadsignmath.xyz](https://roadsignmath.xyz/) became the map of every sign, the blog retired on October 30, 2026, and the next day the app [went to Apple](https://github.com/jthingelstad/roadsignmath.xyz/pull/22) for a public beta. [“It’s entirely different because of AI, because I could not create the experience that I always envisioned creating.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q10)

The point system from the first site did not come back. “The point system wasn’t a good idea. So I want to keep to the root which is a fun road trip game based on math and finding signs.” There are badges instead, little road signs for firsts and places, and no ranks.

Twenty-one years, three sites, one game.
