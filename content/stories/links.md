Jamie has been saving links since August 2005, and three times he has built a place of his own to keep them. [“Having that published as a database has always been a wish of mine.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q11)

The first was links_thing, in 2012. After months of thinking about hosting his own bookmarks he built a wiki on Semantic MediaWiki where [every bookmark was a page](https://www.thingelstad.com/2012/07/01/bookmarking-with-semantic.html). A bookmarklet filled in the title and excerpt, a form made entry easy, and he imported just under 4,000 links from Pinboard. Owning the data was the whole point. “It is mine,” he wrote. He added views no bookmarking service offered, like bookmarks on a calendar, and a [35-line Python bot](https://www.thingelstad.com/projects/open-loop/0-but-no-shoes/) that checked five random bookmarks an hour and stripped tracking codes. By 2014 the wiki had gone quiet, and he was saving to Pinboard again when the Weekly Thing started in 2017. That collection, he said later, [“that was really the backbone for the Weekly Thing.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q11)

The second was Link Thing, a link blog. The links he saved for the Weekly Thing already had Markdown in their Pinboard descriptions, and the same Markdown could become Jekyll posts. He put it live in [September 2017](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/18/), added [search over 9,000 links](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/21/) three weeks later, moved it to [Blot](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/88/) in 2019, and [relaunched it](https://www.thingelstad.com/2020/11/08/i-got-my.html) in November 2020 to give the links “a permanent home on the web.” The last copy in the Internet Archive is from December 2021. The site is gone now. Of the two early runs: [“I tried various versions. And none have really landed and been the thing that I really envisioned they could be.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q11)

In January 2024 he wrote in [Weekly Thing 274](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/274/) that if he ever made a link blog again, he wanted it “to not be blog-date-centric but instead content-keyword-search centric.” That is the sentence Linking Things started from. He built it on October 23, 2026, with coding agents: every link he has saved since 2005, 16,524 of them from 7,064 hosts, one page per link, search first, and the Weekly Thing issue that carried each one. Every link has a JSON and a Markdown twin, every host has feeds of its own, and there is no server behind any of it. [“I think the current version is what I really hope that it could be.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q11)

Each run kept something from the one before. The wiki was about owning the data. The blog showed that dates are the wrong spine for links. Linking Things is built on both. And the reason for all of it is the hyperlink. As he put it in [Weekly Thing 352](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/352/), “Long live the hyperlink!” In the interview he went back further:

> I love the open web. … I still remember the very first version of Mosaic that I ever used. Was unique in that every web server that existed in the world was pre pre-populated in the bookmarks. That’s how early I was that I was able to see the web. So bookmarking on the web has just been something I’ve done forever.
>
> [Jamie, question 11](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q11)
