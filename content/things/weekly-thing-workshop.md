After nine years of Weekly Things assembled with Shortcuts, Jamie wanted help that didn’t wait for him. The old workflow “only runs when I engage with it. It is also brittle and ‘one way’.” In May 2026 he introduced [the Weekly Thing Team](https://www.thingelstad.com/2026/05/12/the-weekly-thing-team.html), “the genesis of my Workshop,” four agents on Discord, each with the whole Weekly Thing archive as a tool:

- **Eddy**, the editor, assembled a working draft of the current issue every day and reviewed it.
- **Linky**, the researcher, scouted the links Jamie flagged. When he replied with his commentary, Linky synced it back to Pinboard.
- **Marky** worked on bringing the newest issue to new readers.
- **Patty** ran the Supporting Membership calls to action, and was the only agent whose drafts appeared in an issue.

“My words are mine!” Jamie wrote. The agents were a support team, not writers. [Weekly Thing 349](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/349/), sent on May 23, 2026, was the first issue made start to finish in the Workshop, “by far the biggest change I’ve ever made to the Weekly Thing automation.” Then over the summer, as he put it in [Weekly Thing 350](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/350/), his work on an Agent Studio “kind of blew up my own publishing process.” That issue was the first made in WT Builder, which has made every issue since. The Workshop lasted four months. In the interview, where he calls it the Studio, he remembers it as a tool that never finished an issue: [“the workshop never actually generated an issue of the Weekly Thing”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q13). Weekly Thing 349 says otherwise. Both are on the record here. What it taught him:

> that was an example where I felt like I could use AI coding tools and maybe create anything. So I didn’t really know what I wanted. I kind of framed it up as this idea that if I had a creative studio filled with agents that could work on projects alongside me, that I think that could be really powerful. Ultimately, it never worked. I tried having a number of different agents working, trying having them collaborate. I never was able to get that to produce anything of value, and I ended up abandoning it very quickly. Because it just always became overly complicated.
>
> [Jamie, question 27](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q27)

The replacement went the other way: [“WT Builder and the weekly website, those were the better solutions, and then build a thin agentic layer on top, which WT Builder has”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q27).
