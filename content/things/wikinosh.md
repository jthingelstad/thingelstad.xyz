Wikinosh was a wiki of nutrition facts. Each food had a page with a nutrition label (serving size, calories, fat, sodium, carbohydrates, and protein) stored as Semantic MediaWiki properties, and foods were grouped into categories like Baby Foods, Beverages, and Spices and Herbs. By [December 2011](https://web.archive.org/web/20111205190345/http://wikinosh.com/wiki/Main_Page) it had 13,753 food records, and by [mid-2012](https://web.archive.org/web/20120630194132/http://wikinosh.com/wiki/Main_Page) 21,550. In January 2013 [WikiApiary](https://web.archive.org/web/20130103025340/http://wikiapiary.com/wiki/Wikinosh) counted 20,433 articles and 981,167 property values on it. A ton of data for a hobby wiki.

On [Open Loop #2](https://www.thingelstad.com/projects/open-loop/2-what-am-i-editing-anyway/) in June 2012, I described the food wiki I had “kind of in alpha” and how turning on Wikimedia Commons let a page show a photo of a banana just by naming the file. In July 2013 I worked with Yaron Koren to put Wikinosh’s data into his Miga app for offline, mobile access, and wrote up the Semantic MediaWiki limits I hit exporting more than 20,000 items ([Paging Large Datasets in Semantic MediaWiki](https://www.thingelstad.com/2013/07/30/paging-large-datasets.html)).

The @Wikinosh Twitter account posted a random food from its 28,000-plus entries every six hours. In November 2013 one of those posts, about trout, drew an IRC-style trout slap from another bot, which still makes me laugh ([Slapped with a Large Trout](https://www.thingelstad.com/2013/11/04/slapped-with-a.html)). The last copy of the wiki in the Internet Archive is from April 2014, and by November 2014 I listed @Wikinosh among the Twitter accounts I had deleted ([Twitter Spam #NFL](https://www.thingelstad.com/2014/11/28/twitter-spam-nfl.html)).
