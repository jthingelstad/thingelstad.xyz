The Charlie Brown Tree, which Jamie’s family also calls the Peanuts Tree, is a tall tree stump at Magic Pines, their place on Cannon Lake, carved with Peanuts characters climbing toward Snoopy’s red doghouse on top. Adam Gale of [Rust n’ Grain](https://rustngraincom.wordpress.com/) carved it. Jamie posted [the carving in progress](https://www.thingelstad.com/2020/05/24/little-snippet-of.html), Charlie Brown holding Snoopy emerging from the wood, on May 24, 2020, and the tree in the snow in [Weekly Thing 163](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/163/) that October. The [project page](https://www.thingelstad.com/magic-pines/charlie-brown-tree/) has videos.

The carving was Tammy’s idea. [“The tree sadly died, and we were trying to figure out what to do with it. She loved the idea of having a carving done, and she found the artist.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-8/#q56) The design was Adam Gale’s: [“He sketched it out on paper, and then he made a three-dimensional clay model of what the tree would look like. We still have it, framed as a diorama”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-8/#q57). The family weighed in on Snoopy and his house at the top, and on the details:

> And there are Easter eggs. The tree has references to objects that are very much about our family. There are books on a shelf that our kids really liked. Instead of holding a football, one character has a soccer ball, because we’re into soccer. One of the characters is holding a Kubb piece. So it’s Peanuts, but it’s also personalized to us and the things we do.
>
> [Jamie, question 56](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-8/#q56)

The logs left over from the tree became Escher-inspired [carved log tables](https://www.thingelstad.com/2021/06/12/i-love-our.html) for the fire area in 2021, another Rust n’ Grain project. In May 2025 he made the tree the site of [his first location-based POAP](https://www.thingelstad.com/2025/05/25/i-created-my-very-first.html), which visitors could mint by standing beside it.

It is archived because it is finished. In the interview he called it [“a very finite project”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-4/#q28), carved [“out of a dead tree at our cabin”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-4/#q28), and [“an amazing piece of art”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-4/#q28).
