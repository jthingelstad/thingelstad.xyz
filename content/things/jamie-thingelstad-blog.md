Jamie had a website before he had a blog. He [counts June 2, 2004](https://www.thingelstad.com/2025/01/12/if-i-was-born-and.html) as the blog’s official start, the date of its [first post](https://www.thingelstad.com/2004/06/02/expose-of-the.html), and the archive also holds posts dated as far back as 2000 that he added to it later. The habit has lasted more than 20 years and over 10,000 posts. The blog is personal and noncommercial, and it is the center of everything else on this site. He says so himself: it is [“probably the root of everything”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q25) and [“the project that is the anchor of every other project”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q25).

> I always tell them that the key, in my opinion, of a blog is that you have to be comfortable writing it for really if it’s just you reading it. And an amazing thing about my blog is that after I have created You know, so many years of posts, it’s become something that I treasure and value for what it is. You could argue that in a way it’s a public journal.
>
> [Jamie, question 25](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q25)

The blog has moved platforms several times. It started on .Text, a Windows .NET blogging package, and [moved to WordPress](https://www.thingelstad.com/2006/11/25/migrating-text-to.html) in 2006. Apart from a [brief move to Pelican](https://www.thingelstad.com/2014/03/16/i-flopped-httpstcozmirtevd.html) in March 2014, it stayed on WordPress until April 2016, when he [moved it to Squarespace](https://www.thingelstad.com/2016/04/20/moved-my-blog.html), and then to [Jekyll](https://www.thingelstad.com/2017/10/29/managing-years-of.html), hosted first on GitHub Pages and then [on Netlify](https://www.thingelstad.com/2018/12/29/macro-blog.html). He began using Micro.blog in April 2017. In May 2020 he [imported the whole Jekyll site](https://www.thingelstad.com/2020/05/02/importing-jekyll-site.html) into it, more than 1,600 posts and 800MB of images, and Micro.blog has been the blog’s home ever since. The archive has stayed in one piece through every move, which he counts as unusual.

Over the years the writing grew from mostly longer articles to include short updates and, more and more, photos, as phones made posting easy. His blog posts from each week are also republished in the Weekly Thing. [“I really think of every time I write a blog post, I think of it as giving myself a present in the future.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q25) It was the first thing he named when asked what he hopes is still running in twenty years: [“For sure, my blog.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q35)
