Road Sign Math is a driving game: find a road sign whose numbers make a valid equation, photograph it, and share it. It began with a sign on I-94 East toward the Twin Cities that Jamie passed coming home from visiting family in North Dakota, which listed distances of 3, 33 and 99 miles: 3 times 33 equals 99. On [Open Loop #1](https://www.thingelstad.com/projects/open-loop/1-big-fuzzy-dead-cat/) Jamie recalled that his friend Jim told him he should make a website, “and so that day I did a website.”

Jamie launched the site in February 2005 as a blog of signs, and other people sent in their own, so it gained rules, a scoring system and scoreboards for players and states. Signs came from all 50 states and most continents, including one from the Serengeti; by 2012 there were over 200. In 2011 he moved it from a blog to a wiki so players could maintain their own signs; an iOS app for capturing signs was considered but never built. In 2016, during a round of [technical downsizing](https://www.thingelstad.com/2016/05/03/technical-downsizing.html), he shut the site down and let roadsignmath.com expire ([Road Sign Math Rebooted](https://www.thingelstad.com/2023/10/21/road-sign-math.html)).

In 2017 Jamie started a Jekyll site for the signs, [rsm.thingelstad.com](https://github.com/jthingelstad/rsm.thingelstad.com), on a stock theme. Jamie registered roadsignmath.xyz in 2021 and in October 2023 [rebooted](https://www.thingelstad.com/2023/10/21/road-sign-math.html) Road Sign Math there as a simple blog: “Just my signs. No scoring system or leaderboards.” New signs still appear, such as one in [September 2025](https://www.thingelstad.com/2025/09/01/it-always-makes-me-smile.html).
