The 612 POAP Challenge was a citywide scavenger hunt that ran from the end of May through Labor Day 2025. [How to Play](https://612.poapchallenge.com/how-to-play/) listed 31 Minneapolis locations in five themed categories, each with a location POAP that could only be claimed in the POAP app within 100 meters of the spot. Two hidden easter-egg locations were left for the curious. Completing a category earned a Category Collector POAP, and collecting all 31 earned the top award.

I [announced it on May 31, 2025](https://www.thingelstad.com/2025/05/31/poap-challenge-collect-them-now.html). It joined two things I love: geocaching, and the [612 Series](https://www.thingelstad.com/2023/04/05/complete-collection-of.html) of Minneapolis NFTs by Erik Halaas and StayNftyMpls, which I collected in full and whose creator I had [interviewed](https://www.thingelstad.com/2024/01/15/interview-with-series.html) in 2024. Six days earlier I had made [my first location POAP](https://www.thingelstad.com/2025/05/25/i-created-my-very-first.html), at our family’s Charlie Brown Tree, and I wanted to build something bigger with the new feature. The challenge’s tokens were illustrated in the spirit of the 612 Series. “I love that I was able to create an entire activity on the POAP platform.”

The challenge had its own Buttondown mailing list, and from July its site, hosted on Micro.blog, posted each claim as it happened through [POAP2RSS](https://www.thingelstad.com/2025/07/06/with-the-help-of-poaprss.html). The [first Collected Them All! award](https://www.thingelstad.com/2025/07/20/we-have-our-very-first.html) went on July 20, 2025 to @quinntchrest, who [speed ran](https://quinnchrest.com/2025/07/20/poap-challenge.html) the challenge by bike with a friend. Amazing. The challenge closed as Labor Day ended on September 1, 2025, and the final awards went out a few days later. The site remains as its record, and the whole experience led me to set up poapchallenge.com for future challenges.
