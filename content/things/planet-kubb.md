Planet Kubb began in 2012, when Jamie bought planetkubb.com and set it up as a planet-style aggregator of Kubb content, built on rawdog with a Bootstrap theme, that collected Kubb videos from YouTube and posts from club blogs. More sites followed: Ask Planet Kubb for questions and answers, the [Planet Kubb Wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/20120512222332/http://wiki.planetkubb.com/wiki/Main_Page) for clubs, teams and tournaments, and The Pitch, a WordPress P2 chat site at pitch.planetkubb.com that Jamie put up within a day of a player from Des Moines asking for one. He told the story on [Open Loop #1](https://www.thingelstad.com/projects/open-loop/1-big-fuzzy-dead-cat/), and said he had gone to great lengths to show it was not an attempt “to monetize the Kubb universe.” The [Planet Kubb GitHub organization](https://github.com/Planet-Kubb) describes the aim as “growing, connecting and supporting the worldwide Kubb community.”

Jamie played Kubb on the Kubbchucks. At the [2013 US Nationals](https://www.thingelstad.com/2013/07/21/pictures-from-us.html) he spent Sunday “scoring games for Planet Kubb and running the final parts of the Planet Kubb Club Championship.” Club sites joined the network, such as the [Chippewa Valley Kubb League](https://www.thingelstad.com/2013/10/06/another-planetkubb-network.html) in 2013, and with a friend Jamie created a notation and scoring system for recording games (see Planet Kubb Score).

planetkubb.com is still online as an aggregator, now [maintained by Garrick van Buren](https://planetkubb.com/about/); its feed was last updated in June 2017. The wiki, Ask Planet Kubb and the club subdomains no longer answer, and survive in Wayback Machine captures.
