By early 2010 Jamie had a portfolio site at photography.thingelstad.com. The [oldest copy in the Internet Archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20100224054709/http://photography.thingelstad.com/), from February 2010, sorts his pictures into Featured, Garden, Outdoor and Travel, with type served by Kernest, Garrick van Buren’s web font service.

In July 2012 he [relaunched it](https://www.thingelstad.com/2012/07/01/website-for-my.html) on WordPress with the AutoFocus theme: “I’ve got a new website where I am highlighting my photography, Thingelstad Photography!” The plan was “an image a week, or more” from his Lightroom archive.

What the portfolio was for, in his words:

> There was a time when I took a lot of photo workshops, and I always had the idea of using a website as a portfolio. I would create the portfolio, and then I’d need to fill it. It was a way to create a demand, and then I’d have to go and photograph to fill that demand. Garden, for example, was one of the workshops I took: garden photography.
>
> [Jamie, question 45](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-7/#q45)

He sees the same habit in the photo that opens each Weekly Thing: [“I need to come up with one photo a week that I believe deserves to be shared and highlighted, and that’s the demand I create to put my creative energy toward.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-7/#q45)

The site went away quietly at some point. He never wrote about shutting it down, and of the smaller projects of those years he said, [“Most of those I don’t have much fondness for”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-4/#q26).
