The Tetris Shower is the downstairs bathroom shower in Jamie’s house, retiled in 2009 as a game in progress. After twenty years the house had settled enough to crack the old tile. [His post](https://www.thingelstad.com/2009/05/03/tetris-shower.html) says a friend helping Tammy with the design drew up a Tetris concept, and “I loved it right away!”

The rules were his part. “It was critical to me that the tile pattern be correct.” Every piece had to be whole, and no row could be complete, because the game would have removed it. To get boards like that, he played several games of Quinn, a tetromino game for the Mac, and captured the screens. He had to learn to play “specifically to not complete rows.” Then, he said, [“I put multiple stacks of games next to each other, all the while making sure I never completed a row.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-8/#q55)

Why the game had to be right:

> Because if the game wasn’t right, the whole story would fall apart. You can’t have a Tetris shower and then have something in the tiles that isn’t a valid tetromino, or that wouldn’t happen in the game.
>
> [Jamie, question 55](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-8/#q55)

Terry Owens of Como Lake Carpentry laid the tile. Jamie’s part in a thing someone else’s hands make, as he put it: [“The creative inspiration, the creative direction, the visual aesthetics.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/session-8/#q61) The top rows are empty because the game is still going, and a single piece is on its way down.

It is archived because it is finished. Eleven years later, [Jamie wrote](https://www.thingelstad.com/2020/05/03/the-tetris-shower.html) that it “is still one of my favorite home projects we’ve done.”
