VeloTweets, “the Pulse of the Peloton,” collected the Twitter posts of professional road cyclists in one place. In early 2009 Jamie had started following riders such as Lance Armstrong through their RSS feeds and noticed that, read together, they gave a first-person view of the peloton during races. VeloTweets included only pro riders and a few others in the sport, added each rider’s team and nationality, and showed each rider once in a grid with their most recent message, so frequent posters did not crowd out everyone else. It also gathered TwitPic photos as VeloPics, kept a race calendar, and ran a Message of the Day tied to the day’s racing.

It was a side project with no budget and no business behind it. The idea was Jamie’s. Luke Francl did most of the development, in Rails 2.3; Norm Orstad designed the site; Chris Hatch built the list of riders and filled in their profiles; and Jamie “played sweeper,” helping wherever he could. The grid came out of a working session: Jamie credits Norm’s comparison to a baseball card, and Luke credits Jamie with showing one tweet per rider. Luke [announced the site on Rail Spikes](https://web.archive.org/web/20090514074605/http://railspikes.com/2009/5/10/announcing-velotweets) on May 10, 2009, and Jamie followed two days later with [Introducing VeloTweets](https://www.thingelstad.com/2009/05/12/introducing-velotweets-the.html) and [Why do Cyclists Tweet?](https://www.thingelstad.com/2009/05/12/why-do-cyclists-tweet.html).

The last Internet Archive capture of the working site is from [March 2010](https://web.archive.org/web/20100318125905/http://www.velotweets.com/); by [February 2011](https://web.archive.org/web/20110202042105/http://www.velotweets.com/) velotweets.com was a parked domain. In 2014 Jamie listed @VeloTweets among the Twitter accounts he had deleted ([Twitter Spam #NFL](https://www.thingelstad.com/2014/11/28/twitter-spam-nfl.html)).
