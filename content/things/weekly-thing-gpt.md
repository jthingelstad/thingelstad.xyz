On February 25, 2025, Jamie [made a custom GPT](https://www.thingelstad.com/2025/02/25/weekly-thing-gpt.html) that anyone with a ChatGPT account could use, “loaded with all 310 issues of the Weekly Thing published so far.” He suggested questions like “What is the Weekly Thing view on social media?”, “What is worse, Facebook or TikTok?”, and “Show me a list of articles on X along with the issue they were in,” and shared it in [Weekly Thing 311](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/311/).

It had real limits. In March 2025 he showed it [getting the answer wrong](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/313/) when he asked which issue the Fortune first appeared in. Fourteen months later he launched Thingy, which actually searches the archive and cites its sources, and compared the two in [Weekly Thing 347](https://weekly.thingelstad.com/archive/347/): the GPT “was grade school level. Thingy is much smarter!” The GPT’s link no longer works. What it was good for, in his words:

> it taught me that just sending a bunch of data into that system both was not going to give me the kind of experience that I wanted, but it gave me enough of a taste that there was something here and that if I could build a real agent around it, that that could be meaningful.
>
> [Jamie, question 29](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q29)
