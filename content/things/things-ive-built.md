thingelstad.xyz is where Jamie keeps the point-in-time things he makes, and this site is the index of all of them. Every project, active or long retired, with its sources, how it connects to the other projects, and a sourced history of what happened when. The registry is Markdown and YAML in a public repository. Evidence comes in every day from GitHub, sitemaps, feeds, the Internet Archive, and Tinylytics, and the whole thing builds to a static site with an RSS feed and llms.txt.

He started the repository in October 2026 as a project gallery with an AI guide. On [October 17, 2026](https://github.com/jthingelstad/thingelstad.xyz/pull/6) it became a static site built with Eleventy from the Markdown registry, and the AI guide, private editor and React application were [retired](https://github.com/jthingelstad/thingelstad.xyz/pull/8). Thingy’s archive [indexes](https://github.com/jthingelstad/thingelstad.xyz/pull/9) the published things.json, so you can ask Thingy about any of these projects. Since November 2026 Thingy has kept the site, and the [about page](https://thingelstad.xyz/about/) explains how that works.

What the site is for, in Jamie’s words: [“a reminder of all the amazing things that I’ve created over the years”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q7), and [“an archive and a history of all of the things that I’ve created. So that that can all connect together.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q7) On seeing the first version:

> it made the threads and eras more visible. Like, like I know that I had a MediaWiki era or a wiki era in general. I knew that I had a crypto era in general. And I knew that the blog was the backbone of all of it, and it made that a lot more obvious. There were a handful of projects like VeloTweets that I had totally forgotten about, that, It was delightful to see that come back to life.
>
> [Jamie, question 34](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q34)

And the name. The things here are not a portfolio or a business, [“that’s kind of why I ended up calling it things I built”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q8). [“It’s just the act of building and creating.”](https://thingelstad.xyz/interviews/jamie-october-2026/#q8)
